    Help & Rules

    Using this game

    • All games (top link) returns to the home screen where you can pick Chess or Sudoku.
    • Select a piece by clicking it. Legal squares light up with a dot (or a ring for captures).
    • Move by clicking a highlighted square.
    • vs Computer — you play White; the computer plays Black and thinks briefly between moves.
    • 2 Players — take turns on the same device.
    • New game starts a fresh match. Your hardness level is kept.
    • Undo takes back the last move (and the computer’s reply in vs Computer).
    • Flip board rotates the board view.
    • Hardness goes up one level each time you beat the computer. It stays raised until you press Reset level.

    Board cues

    • Gold highlight = last move.
    • Red outline on king = that side is in check.
    • Move list on the right shows standard chess notation (SAN).

    How each piece moves

    ♔ / ♚ King

    One square in any direction. Cannot move into check. Castling is a special king move (see Special rules).

    ♕ / ♛ Queen

    Any number of squares vertically, horizontally, or diagonally — like rook + bishop combined.

    ♖ / ♜ Rook

    Any number of squares vertically or horizontally. Involved in castling.

    ♗ / ♝ Bishop

    Any number of squares diagonally. Stays on the same color square forever.

    ♘ / ♞ Knight

    L-shape: 2 squares one way and 1 sideways (or 1 and 2). Jumps over pieces.

    ♙ / ♟ Pawn

    Forward one square (two from the starting rank). Captures one square diagonally forward. Can promote on the last rank.

    White pieces start on ranks 1–2; Black on ranks 7–8. White always moves first.

    Special rules (all enforced here)

    Check & checkmate

    If your king is attacked, you are in check and must escape (move the king, block, or capture the attacker). If you cannot escape, it is checkmate and you lose.

    Stalemate

    If it is your turn, you are not in check, but have no legal moves, the game is a draw by stalemate.

    Castling (O-O / O-O-O)

    Move the king two squares toward a rook, and the rook jumps to the far side of the king. Allowed only if:

    • King and that rook have not moved yet
    • Squares between them are empty
    • King is not in check, and does not pass through or land on an attacked square

    En passant

    If an enemy pawn jumps two squares and lands beside yours, you may capture it on the next move as if it had moved only one square. This must be done immediately.

    Pawn promotion

    When a pawn reaches the far rank, choose Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight. The computer always promotes to a Queen.

    50-move rule

    If 50 full moves pass with no capture and no pawn move, the game is drawn.

    Helpful tips

    • Control the center — e4, d4, e5, d5 give your pieces more room.
    • Develop early — bring out knights and bishops before launching big attacks.
    • Castle early — get the king safe and connect your rooks.
    • Don’t hang pieces — before you move, check whether that piece can be taken for free.
    • Look at checks — forcing moves (checks and captures) often reveal tactics.
    • Trade when ahead — if you are up material, simplifying can make the win easier.
    • Use Undo wisely — great for learning; try a line, undo, and try another.
    • Climb hardness slowly — win at Beginner, then Easy, and so on. Reset level if the computer becomes too strong.
    • Watch the move list — learning SAN (Nf3, O-O, exd5) helps you follow chess anywhere.

    Quick notation guide

    • K, Q, R, B, N = king, queen, rook, bishop, knight (pawns have no letter)
    • x = capture · + = check · # = checkmate
    • O-O = kingside castle · O-O-O = queenside castle
    • =Q = pawn promoted to queen
